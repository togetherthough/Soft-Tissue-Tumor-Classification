• Heterogeneous group of lesions, the diagnosis often requires invasive biopsy because many 
tumors look similar on imaging [Fletcher, 2018]
• Key clinical challenge: distinguishing benign from malignant subtypes non-invasively e.g. lipoma 
vs well-differentiated liposarcoma appears nearly identical on MRI
• Machine learning can help, but faces three barriers: (1) small labeled datasets, (2) high 
computational cost of 3D volumes, (3) need for generalizable features across scanners and 
protocols

SAM-Med3D architecture
• Encoder output (384 × 8 × 8 × 8) must be compressed into a 
fixed-size vector per case using a pooling layer + PCA
• Three pooling strategies compared, all with TabPFN + ROI 
cropping + 5-fold CV (Experiment 4):
• Average pooling: global mean → 384-dim vector (no PCA needed)
• Multiscale pooling: adaptive average at 1³, 2³, 4³ resolutions → 
concatenate → 28,032-dim (PCA to ≤500)
• Percentile pooling:10th, 25th, 50th, 75th, 90th percentile per 
channel → 5 × 384 = 1,920-dim (PCA to ≤500)

TabPFN architecture
• LoCalPFN = Locally Calibrated PFN — extends TabPFN with 
local neighborhood retrieval [Thomas et al., 2024]
• Motivation: tumors have subtypes that occupy different regions of 
feature space with different decision boundaries — a global 
classifier may miss these [Atkeson et al., 1997]

TRANSFER LEARNING EXPERIMENT
22/02/2026
Footer
16
• The goal is to determine whether the frozen encoder is even 
"looking at" the tumor without even taking into consideration 
the classifier
• This has been achieved by capturing the self-attention 
weights from all 12 ViT-B blocks using monkey-patching and 
computing multiplicative rollout across the 4 global attention 
blocks (2, 5, 8, 11) with residual mixing
• The metrics collected are attention mass (fraction of attention 
inside lesion mask) and concentration ratio (attention mass ÷ 
lesion volume fraction; >1 = above chance)
