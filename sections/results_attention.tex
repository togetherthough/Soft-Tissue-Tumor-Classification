\subsection{Attention Map Analysis}\label{sec:res_attention}

Attention rollout across all 930 cases confirms that the frozen SAM-Med3D
encoder preferentially attends to lesion regions without task-specific
supervision, achieving a mean concentration ratio of $4.20\times$ (well
above the chance level of $1.0$).

The bias varies by dataset. CRLM and Liver show the strongest concentration
($5.58\times$ and $5.16\times$) despite small lesions ($0.50\%$ and $1.25\%$
of volume), suggesting the encoder localizes compact, well-defined lesions
most effectively. GIST and Lipo exhibit moderate concentration ($3.78\times$
and $3.89\times$) with larger lesions that dilute the ratio. Melanoma is a
clear outlier ($1.76\times$, near chance), likely reflecting its diffuse
metastatic appearance on CT.

Rollout consistently outperforms single-block attention ($4.20\times$ vs.\
$3.13\times$ for the last block), confirming that lesion-relevant attention
accumulates across depth. The scatter plot
(Fig.~\ref{fig:attn_scatter}) shows nearly all cases above the $y{=}x$
diagonal, meaning the encoder allocates more attention than volume alone
would predict. However, attending to the lesion is necessary but not
sufficient — the encoder must also encode \emph{label-discriminative}
features within those regions (Section~\ref{sec:res_diagnostics}).
